The history of the modular synthesizer

Modular synthesizers emerged in the late 1950s and 1960s as electronic composers and engineers sought to build flexible instruments from discrete, patchable components. Early pioneers included Robert Moog and Don Buchla, each of whom developed influential voltage-controlled systems on opposite coasts of the United States.

Moog's systems, based on the 1/V-per-octave standard, were oriented toward keyboard performance and tonal composition. Buchla's systems emphasized the Buchla Box's unusual touchplate controllers and experimental, non-keyboard-centric approaches to sound design.

Interest in modular synthesis waned during the 1980s with the rise of affordable polyphonic digital synths, but was revived in the 1990s and 2000s with the Eurorack format introduced by Doepfer. Today modular synthesis is a thriving niche, with hundreds of small manufacturers producing compatible modules.
