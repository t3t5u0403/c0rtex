The history of the modular synthesizer

Modular synthesizers emerged in the late 1950s and 1960s as electronic composers sought flexible, patchable instruments. Robert Moog and Don Buchla were early pioneers on opposite coasts of the United States.

Moog's systems used the 1/V-per-octave standard and were oriented toward keyboard performance. Buchla's systems emphasized touchplate controllers and non-keyboard-centric sound design.

Interest in modular synthesis was revived in the 1990s with Doepfer's Eurorack format. Today, modular synthesis is a thriving niche.
